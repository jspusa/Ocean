# 0.18.52 — roof-query CPU work and portable chunk caching

The fish update repeatedly asks for a vertical ceiling height. The previous implementation used the general convex sweep, constructing origin/direction/result arrays and contact normals that the caller discarded. This release specializes that one query. It preserves axis order, directed entry/exit limits, plane arithmetic (including zero products), tolerances, the finite-origin fallback and final height reduction. General sphere sweeps and their contact normals are unchanged.

No runtime renderer, shader, authored model, texture, LOD range, animal inventory, update cadence, resolution or adaptive-quality policy changes are included. Scene gzip metadata is also made portable as described below. This is a bounded optimization, not completion of the smooth-play or Wii-parity objective. Physical phone FPS, sustained play and battery consumption remain unverified.

## Deterministic regression

`node tests/parity/region-query-performance.cjs` is RED on the pre-fix query: 1,280 sampled rays enter the general interval routine 43,623 times and allocate 586 discarded plane-normal slices. Both counts are zero after the fix. The independent frozen reference still checks exact heights, roof boundaries, negative bucket boundaries, directed/zero rays and unusual numeric origins. General candidate Set order and the immutable roof-column index remain unchanged. Test instrumentation must match exactly one actual interval entry point.

`node tests/parity/ceiling-life-cpu.cjs --before-ref=a30a06cba1c98d8cc085dd9cd90518e4cab011e6` compares fresh worlds using the same current archive/modules and only substitutes the baseline `regions.js`. It retains raw timing samples and checks complete animal state plus rock-query/build/node/triangle counts after eight alternating rounds of 60 updates, then repeated and rewound times. Timing has no pass/fail threshold.

The initial equivalent scratch replay covered 445 archived animals and 380 school fish. Its observed upper-middle CPU samples were 16.223 → 14.976 ms/update with VM-local Math, and 11.757 → 11.365 ms/update with explicitly shared host Math. These environments have different overhead; the measurements fluctuate and do not establish a whole-game FPS improvement.

After the complete rebuild, the tracked command passes against the final archive with exact complete state and contact counts. Observed medians are 15.151 → 13.639 ms/update (VM-local Math) and 11.022 → 10.459 ms/update (host Math). These remain CPU-only samples with no statistical or device-FPS claim; all samples are retained in `qa/parity/ceiling-life-cpu.json`.

## Browser CPU differential

`PLAYWRIGHT_PATH=... node tests/parity/ceiling-life-browser.cjs --before=http://127.0.0.1:8766/ --after=http://127.0.0.1:8765/` is a repeatable browser fixture. It boots both real applications, matches optional fish readiness and pins actual loaded `regions.js` response hashes to separately fetched bytes and the executing function. Rasterization is guarded during the CPU replay; native browser Math is used.

The pre-release run passed 13 forward/repeated/rewound complete-state snapshots for 446 animals (including the loaded optional barramundi) and 380 school fish, plus 11,433 exact roof queries covering 3,884 roof solids and 99 selected boundary solids. The final complete state also matches after all timed rounds. Nonfinite values, undefined values and negative zero are preserved by snapshot encoding. Both contexts report no GL error or context loss, and no render call occurs during the CPU fixture.

After two warm rounds, six alternating rounds of 120 actual updates produced observed medians of 1.9817 → 1.9367 ms/update (2.27% lower). All raw samples are retained. Some candidate rounds are slower; this small sample is not a statistically established speedup, rendered FPS, physical phone result or battery measurement. Deterministic discarded-work removal and exact behavior are the primary evidence.

## Rejected rendering experiment

A scratch fragment-only soft-coral (`kind=3`, `material=7`) specialization retained exact whole-frame RGBA, drawing, geometry, uploads and residency on both software-WebGL profiles. Three alternating query pairs per profile gave complete-frame medians of 5388.11 → 5339.04 ms (desktop) and 5095.24 → 4979.57 ms (touch emulation). These are SwiftShader software-GPU timings, not device FPS. The small observed differences do not justify another startup shader program; the experiment is not shipped.

## Release validation

The full scene build completed, including the 916.9-second world/root-fitting phase. Its archive SHA-256 is `d39f92bd48b060f0499094f1b92f2c6da527b1fa5a5949709fac52a4d901367c` (25,296,584 compressed / 66,444,704 decoded bytes). It retains 509 batches, 458 streamed meshes, 185 chunks and 3,729 shared instances. This is a rebuilt archive; it is **not byte-identical** to the published v0.18.51 archive.

Independent structured comparisons find exact indices/order, positions, colors, UVs, tags and metadata for every mesh; 507 meshes are entirely byte-identical. Sixteen near-zero normal components in low turtle/cuttlefish meshes differ by at most `2.79e-16`. Isolated unmodified v0.18.51 source factories reproduce the rebuilt meshes exactly while ceiling queries are forbidden, proving these normal differences do not depend on the roof optimization. Twenty header numbers differ by at most `1.78e-15`, all rounding to identical Float32 values. Growth content stamp and batch limits match. A complete baseline rebuild was not repeated, so the cause of the tiny header differences is not independently proven. Actual framebuffer comparisons must separately cover these low meshes.

Rebuilding also exposed a real cache portability defect: 183 unchanged geometry chunks had identical deflate payloads but a different gzip OS byte (`19` versus `3`), giving them different URLs. The partitioner now sets the informational OS marker to `255` before hashing. `node tests/parity/scene-partition-portability.cjs` goes RED on the original partitioner and GREEN on the fix: simulated markers 3/19/255 produce exact identical chunk bytes, URLs, hashes, index metadata and decoded geometry; changing one mesh still changes only its chunk URL. The first canonical release has new URLs; future builds can reuse identical chunks across these hosts. This does not reduce geometry, runtime draw work or promise FPS.

The regional contract initially failed its historical 725-actor inventory assertion on both the untouched 0.18.51 checkout and the candidate: 0.18.51 appended two slope lionfish of an existing species. The test now verifies those exact UIDs, species and habitat ownership separately before preserving the original 725-actor inventory and every existing trajectory assertion. Negative controls reject another actor hidden behind a slope label and changed ownership of a known UID. All 17 regional checks pass. The focused roof regression, 16 render-cadence checks and 10 isolated build-input guard checks also pass.

The static fallback check still searched for a literal module name in the compiler after its inputs had moved to `scene_inputs.cjs`. It now checks the actual shared module list and source files; static source/geometry/reference/manifest validation passes. Final framebuffer, swimming and deployed-site verification are recorded after completion. A successful source change or push alone does not establish deployment or real-device smoothness.

The final framebuffer gate passes all eight desktop/touch-profile views: shallow reef, actual hadal depth, and the actual low-LOD turtle/cuttlefish on both profiles. Every full RGBA byte, triangle/draw/shadow count, upload count/bytes and uniform count matches the immutable v0.18.51 baseline. The changed low batches 421/444 must actually submit their target actors. These are explicit 128×96 software-WebGL diagnostic buffers with unchanged quality, scale and MSAA, not native-resolution or physical-phone performance evidence.

Earlier fixture attempts are retained as failures: the historical capture could read a declined/stale frame, and persistent body-contact history prevented a diagnostic jump from reaching its intended destination. The gate now explicitly resets diagnostic placement history, verifies control-camera depth/position and actor low-LOD distance, settles actual-eye geometry/prefetch, and requires complete warm/capture frames with frame advancement before reading pixels. It does not alter production collision, streaming deadlines or required geometry. Cadence is checked by the separate 16-check production-loop suite; the pixel gate explicitly skips the historical cadence probe. Final archive animal-contact (10/10), regional (17/17), streaming eligibility (8/8), gzip portability and static checks also pass.

Both independent Standards and Spec reviews have no unresolved finding. Formal-site acceptance is a separate post-merge check of the Pages commit, every manifest file and real rendered swimming; this pre-merge report makes no claim that a push alone proves publication.
